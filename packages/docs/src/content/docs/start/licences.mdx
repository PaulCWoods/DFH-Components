## The Figma kit

You buy a licence once, and it includes every update to the kit for as long as the kit is sold.

### Who counts

A person counts when they open the kit file in Figma, or edit a file built with its components. People who only view, comment on or inspect the designs, in Dev Mode for example, don't count.

A licence belongs to whoever bought it, a person or an organisation. When someone leaves your team, their place can go to someone else.

### What you can do

- **Use the kit in any number of projects,** your own and your clients', commercial ones included.
- **Hand over design files built with it** as part of a project. Your client can keep, view and share them, but to keep designing with the kit after your project ends, they need their own licence.
- **Change it as you like:** apply your own theme, recolour it, rename things, add components.
- **Build the designs in code** with the free library, under its MIT licence.

### What you can't do

- **Share the kit,** or a changed copy of it: no reselling, giving it away, or posting it publicly or in the Figma Community. Sharing design files you made with it is fine: with your team, your developers or your client. Anyone who edits them counts as one of your licence's people (see [Who counts](#who-counts)).
- **Put the kit in a product,** in whole or in part. A Figma UI kit or design system made from its components, for others to buy or download, is a product. A site and its theme made for one client is a project, so it's fine.
- **Stretch a licence past its number of people:** a Solo licence is for one person, and a Team licence for up to 10.

These limits are on the Figma kit only. The code is under the MIT licence, so you can sell a product built on the CSS library, a WordPress theme for example, as long as the MIT notice stays with the code.

## Pro

Pro is a subscription for each editor. Each editor has their own licence key and keeps it to themselves. The Agency plan is one key shared by up to five editors in one organisation: it works in a limited number of browsers. Removing Pro from a browser frees its place: under **Pro licence** in the Designer menu's **More options**, or in your Polar customer portal for a browser you can't open any more.

- **What you make is yours.** Your themes, and the theme files, CSS and Figma variables the Designer builds from them, are yours to use and license however you like. That covers what the Designer makes from your theme, not the kit's components, which stay under the kit licence above, or the library's code, which stays under the MIT licence.
- **Cancel at any time.** Pro stays on until the end of the period you've paid for. After that your themes stay in your browser and in the files you downloaded, and the free Designer still opens them.

## Icons from others

Most of the icons, in the CSS library's sprite and in the kit, are drawn from [Lucide](https://lucide.dev), under the ISC licence, and some of those come from Feather, under the MIT licence. The brand icons come from [Simple Icons](https://simpleicons.org), which gives them away under CC0.

Their notices are in a comment at the top of the icon sprite, the file `icons.svg` that comes with each version of the library. Keep it in every copy, as with the library's own notice. The icons stay under their own licences wherever they appear, in the kit too: the kit's limits don't extend to them. A brand's logo remains its owner's trademark, and its owner's guidelines say how it may be used.

If you didn't mean to break them, tell us: we'd rather put it right together.
